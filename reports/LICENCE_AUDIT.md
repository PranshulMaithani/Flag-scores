## What the constraint cost us

**Every competitive grammatical-error-correction model on the Hub is non-commercial.** The most widely used one has 86,000 downloads and is CC-BY-NC-SA; the strongest is Grammarly's, also non-commercial; the one with the most downloads of the remaining candidates declares no licence at all.

The only permissively licensed option is `Unbabel/gec-t5_small`, which is a t5-small. Spot-checking confirms it handles the L2 error types that matter (tense, preposition, subject-verb agreement) and leaves correct sentences untouched, so the choice is defensible -- but grammar is the one category where the licence constraint, not the method, sets the ceiling.

If legal will clear a non-commercial model for internal benchmarking only, quantifying that gap would be worthwhile before accepting it.
