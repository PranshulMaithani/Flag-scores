voxscore v0.1.0 · research prototype · 11 Sep 2026

Scoring spoken answers without an LLM

A self-hosted replacement for the assessment scoring vendor. Four scores, four cheating flags, every number traceable to a named feature. Built and measured against public data only — no candidate data was used.

3.1 GB peak VRAM (budget 16) ~1× realtime, all models 91 tests 84 features per response 0 LLM calls
01

Why the vendor scores 0.40 on relevance

The incumbent manages about 0.60 correlation on grammar, lexical and fluency, but only 0.40 on relevance. That asymmetry is not random, and it is the opening.

Grammar, lexical and fluency are properties of the response alone. Relevance is the only category that requires reasoning about the prompt — and the obvious way to do that is nearly uncorrelated with relevance on personal-narrative questions.

Your own ideal answers show why. Three model responses to “Share how you celebrated your most recent birthday.”

Ideal answer 1

“…dinner with my family at home. My mother made one of my favorite meals, and my brother insisted on buying a cake that looked much better than it tasted.”

family · home · a bad cake
Ideal answer 2

“…one friend secretly organized a small evening at a café I really like. When I walked in and saw everyone there, I was genuinely surprised.”

friends · a café · a surprise
Ideal answer 3

“…had a slow morning, got coffee, wandered around a bookshop, then met my sister for dinner. She gave me a notebook filled with little messages.”

alone · a bookshop · a notebook

Content overlap between them is close to zero. All three are perfect answers.

Any system scoring relevance by resemblance to an ideal answer is measuring whose story it is.

We measured the consequence. Against this rubric, a response that does nothing but restate the prompt scored highest of six on raw question similarity — above two genuine answers. A contentless vague answer matched a real one on ideal-answer similarity.

Similarity-based relevance rewards restating the question and saying nothing at length. That alone is enough to produce 0.40.
02

So we score the question, not the answer key

A rubric is derived per question, with no labels and no classifier. Required elements come from the question’s own grammar. Required moves come from whatever two of the three ideal answers agree on after names, places and dates are masked out — two independent answers agreeing makes something a requirement; one mentioning it is personal detail.

How far to trust content matching is decided per question by measuring how much the three ideal answers agree with each other. High for opinion prompts, where model answers converge on the same arguments. Near zero for personal prompts, where they share only structure.

Those are real numbers from a test response: fluent, specific, detailed English prose — about Victorian art criticism, paired with the birthday question. Under an additive model it scored 73/100 while the off-topic flag fired at 98. Multiplying brought it to 8.7.

The bias trap it survives

All three birthday ideal answers describe a low-key celebration. We wrote an adversarial response describing a large, loud party — equally valid, opposite in kind. It scored at or above the quiet one on every relevance feature. A content-matching system would have marked it down for being different. It is now a permanent regression test.

03

The foreign-language flag, calibrated by dose

Rather than a positive/negative set, controlled proportions of Hindi, Tamil, Tagalog, Swahili and Yoruba were spliced into English responses — level-matched and cross-faded, so the detector cannot cheat by hearing the splice. The result is a curve that says what a threshold means.

Why it was capped

The scoring transcript is forced to English on purpose, so a Hindi stretch comes out visibly broken rather than as clean Hindi the grammar and lexical modules would happily score as an answer. But the flag then ran its text language detector over that same forced transcript — which always reads as English. That channel held 30% of the weight and voted “not foreign” on every single item.

The first fix made it worse. Re-transcribing the whole clip with automatic language detection meant Whisper picked the majority language: on a 25%-Hindi response it chose English, correctly, and the score fell from 35.2 to 25.6. The corroborating channel was arguing against the evidence it existed to corroborate.

Now it transcribes only the spans that sounded foreign, and asks whether they actually are.

The relevance gap is the honest headline. We can show the system separates on-topic from off-topic essentially perfectly. We cannot yet show it separates a good on-topic answer from a mediocre one — and that is what a 0–5 relevance label encodes. One run against your held-out set settles it.

Seven bugs, none of which crashed

Every defect found during development produced plausible numbers in a believable range. They are listed because how they were caught transfers better than the fixes do.

Two things that worked better than assertions

Cross-checking independent outputs. The relevance bug surfaced because a flag said off-topic while a score said relevant. Neither number looked wrong on its own.

Controls that must score badly. The confidence bug was caught by scoring silent audio and finding it nearly matched a perfect transcription.

And one class hit three times, now named: a channel that cannot measure must abstain, not vote. Returning zero for “unavailable” reads identically to “measured, and fine”.

What would move this fastest

01

The Ideal Answers sheet as a file

Only the birthday answers were legible in the screenshots. Every other question currently falls back to question-only features, which is measurably weaker. Highest-value single item.

02

A Hugging Face token, and accept the Svarah terms

Unblocks the accented-English fairness audit — the one result standing between the foreign-language flag and deployment.

03

One validation run against your 350

Settles fine-grained relevance, and reveals whether your relevance labels carry a proficiency halo. If they do, that is a weighting change, not a redesign.
